## Why this unit exists — a fabricated entry is already confirmed

`paper/bibliography.bib` was generated by another worker and contains at least one invented paper:

```
@article{cascading_aggregating_kotte_2026,
  title = {Dynamic Cascading and Aggregation for Heterogeneous {LLMs}},
  eprint = {2602.14890}, ...
}
```

arXiv **2602.14890** is really *"Lifted Relational Probabilistic Inference via Implicit Learning"* by
Ge, Juba, Nilsson and Shao — a probabilistic-logic paper with no connection to language models or
agents. The entry appears to have been assembled from an author surname seen in another paper's
reference list, with a plausible title and an unrelated identifier attached.

An identifier that resolves to *some* real paper defeats a naive existence check. **Every entry must
therefore be checked for title-and-author agreement, not merely for resolving.** This matters more
than anything else in the unit: a fabricated citation in a thesis is found by examiners and is
treated as misconduct, not as a slip.

## Scope

Audit **every** entry in `paper/bibliography.bib` (about 59) and every row of

## Method, per entry

1. Fetch the canonical record: `https://arxiv.org/abs/<id>` for arXiv entries, or the ACL
   Anthology / OpenReview / proceedings URL for venue entries.
2. Compare the **title** (ignoring case, punctuation, LaTeX braces) and the **first author surname**
   against the record.
3. Classify into exactly one of:
   - `OK` — identifier resolves and title and first author both match.
   - `MISMATCH` — identifier resolves but the title or first author is different. **Record the real
     title and authors.** This is the fabrication class; the Kotte entry is one.
   - `UNRESOLVED` — the identifier does not resolve at all.
   - `UNVERIFIABLE` — you could not reach the source. Say which source and why. Do **not** guess.
4. For `OK` entries, also record the real submission year and note any year mismatch.

Do not fix anything in place. This unit produces a verdict table; I decide what to delete.
