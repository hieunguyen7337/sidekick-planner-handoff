## A. The closest paper — The Handoff Tax

- **key**: `handoff_tax_ganz_2026`
- **title**: The Handoff Tax: Continuing Non-Native Trajectories in LLM Agents
- **arXiv**: 2608.24358, submitted 25 August 2026
- **authors**: Roy Ganz, Mor Shpigel Nacson, Adi Kalyanpur, Ron Litman
- **url**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24358 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24358v1)

**Abstract (verbatim, complete):**
> "Coding agents perform long-running tasks spanning dozens of model calls, tool uses, and code edits.
> As these runs unfold, users face a practical cost-quality trade-off: escalating to a stronger model
> when a cheaper one struggles, or downshifting once the hard reasoning is complete. Each switch
> requires the receiver to continue a non-native trajectory produced by another model. We study how
> this handoff affects quality and cost, and how varying the trajectory information inherited by the
> receiver changes the outcome. Using pairs of low-cost, low-capability (LC) and high-cost,
> high-capability (HC) models from the Claude and GPT family, we vary handoff direction, timing, and
> interface, comparing full-trajectory transfer, compaction, and trajectory removal while preserving
> the repository state. Across both model families, full-trajectory escalation recovers less than half
> of the LC-to-HC quality gap while incurring a substantial cost premium. We term this cost-quality
> penalty the handoff tax. By contrast, downshift offers a favorable cost-quality point. Interestingly,
> the preferred interface also reverses with direction: reducing LC-model trajectory information
> improves escalation quality, whereas removing the HC-model trajectory reduces downshift quality."

**Setup:**
- Benchmarks: SWE-bench Verified (all 500 instances, primary); Lost in Conversation (535 tasks, 5
  families); BrowseComp (200 questions).
- Model pairs: Claude Haiku 4.5 (LC) / Claude Opus 4.7 (HC); GPT-5.6 Luna (LC) / GPT-5.6 Sol (HC).
  **Note for us: their LC-is-frontier-tier. Our LC is an 8B open-weight local model — a far larger
  capability and cost gap.**
- Switch timing: percentiles of the starting model's step-count distribution, estimated separately
  within difficulty buckets (Easy < 15 min, Medium 15 min–1 h, Hard > 1 h). Verbatim: *"We sweep K
  over percentiles {5,10,15,25,35,45,50}."* and *"Thresholds are fixed before the handoff runs at
  percentiles p∈{5,10,15,25,35,45,50} of the prefix model's single-model termination-step
  distribution."*
- **Results are AVERAGED over switch points**: *"The main results average over all seven switch
  points."* Disaggregation is only into Early (p5–p15) vs Late (p25–p50) windows. **No quality-vs-
  handoff-depth curve is plotted.** ← this is the gap our F1 fills.
- **Receivers are zero-shot.** No fine-tuning, no LoRA, no adaptation anywhere in the paper. ← the gap
  our F4a/F4b fill.
- **The HC model is never used as a critic or advisor**, only as an actor. ← the gap our channel
  contrast fills.
- Four interfaces: `Raw` (full trajectory verbatim, no message announcing the model change);
  `Compact_pre` (prefix model summarises); `Compact_suf` (suffix model summarises); `Traj-drop` (no
  trajectory, only the working tree).
- Statistics: **one episode per task per configuration**; verbatim limitation: *"We run a single
  episode for each of the 500 tasks under every configuration and therefore do not estimate
  variability across repeated runs."* No confidence intervals. ← our pre-registered clustered
  bootstrap with 2 seeds is a methodological upgrade.

**Downshift results (HC→LC, averaged over switch points), Claude pair:**
| metric | LC-only | HC-only | Raw | Compact_pre | Compact_suf | Traj-drop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pass | 54.6% | 75.8% | 65.6% | 66.8% | 63.7% | 60.9% |
| Cost | $0.41 | $0.85 | $0.51 | $0.52 | $0.53 | $0.59 |
| QRec | 0% | 100% | 50% | 56% | 42% | 28% |
| CSRet | 100% | 0% | 80% | 78% | 73% | 59% |

GPT pair: LC 63.6% / HC 85.8% / Raw 81.0%; cost $0.05 / $0.47 / $0.42; QRec 79%; CSRet 14%.

Difficulty-conditional (Claude, Raw): Easy 78.0% (ΔLC +3.0, ΔHC −7.6); Medium 61.8% (+15.2, −9.8);
Hard 35.6% (+20.6, −22.9). Authors call these *"exploratory"* (N̄ ≈ 24–27 per cell on hard).

**Cost measurement**: *"Costs follow provider pricing, including cache reads and writes."* LiteLLM
v1.83.14 price registry. ← our F2 dollar axis should mirror this convention.

**Their stated future work (quote these in our positioning):**
> "Future work can study adaptive, progress-aware policies that jointly decide when to switch and
> which model should continue."
> "Evaluation should also extend across model families, capability gaps, pricing regimes, repeated
> rollouts, and multiple handoffs per trajectory."

**Their related work on mid-trajectory switching** (use for snowballing):
SWE-Router (Son et al., 2026) — uses an LC partial trajectory to decide continue-or-restart, does not
transfer the trajectory; Khraishi et al. 2026 — directional drift when one model continues another's
dialogue prefix, final turn only; KC & Budathoki 2026 — "Handoff Debt", agents taking over interrupted
coding tasks; Zhang et al. 2026a/2026b, Hemadri et al. 2026 — multi-turn routing; cascades/routers:
Chen 2023, Yue 2024, Hu 2024, Ding 2024, Aggarwal 2025, Valkanas 2025, Ong 2025, Kotte 2026.

## B. Replay-handoff as an evaluation protocol

- **key**: `reach_or_solve_2026`
- **title**: Reach or Solve? Attributing Agentic RL Gains with Checkpoint Handoffs
- **arXiv**: 2609.19636v1, 17 September 2026
- **authors**: Xuan Liu (SJTU), Jingbin Qian (Rice)
- **url**: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19636

Introduces **checkpoint handoff**: *"an evaluation protocol that clones a state one released checkpoint
reached and hands it to another, with no retraining."* Roles: *"we assign separate reacher and solver
roles. The reacher produces the state distribution, and the solver acts from a state held fixed."*
Benchmarks ALFWorld (SkillRL) and TravelPlanner (Agent-STAR); Qwen 1.5B/3B/7B. **Both policies are the
same size** — SFT vs RL checkpoints, not a small/large pair. Handoff point is fixed (TravelPlanner:
immediately before the final model call; ALFWorld: an environment-verified frontier D=2 actions from
success), **not swept**. Findings: RL reaches an equally solvable state 6× as often (82.5% vs 13.3%,
ALFWorld unseen); from identical cloned states RL solver 95.7% vs SFT 56.5%; interaction positive in
all five conditions. No FLOPs/token/cost accounting.

**Use**: this is our citation that replay-and-hand-off is an accepted evaluation protocol, and that
restricting to states both policies reach *selects on an outcome* (they report it flips the sign of
the effect) — which is directly relevant to our pinned-key-set analysis.

## C. Prefix replay in TRAINING (not deployment) — the C3 neighbours

- **key**: `reopd_liao_2026` — Multi-Turn On-Policy Distillation with Prefix Replay, arXiv 2607.04763,
  July 2026, Baohao Liao et al., Microsoft. Code: github.com/BaohaoLiao/ReOPD.
  Proposes **ReOPD**: *"reuses pre-collected teacher trajectories as replayed prefixes: the student acts
  at selected steps, while the teacher provides dense per-step supervision without executing new
  environment interactions."* Identifies the **"prefix trap"**: making histories more student-on-policy
  improves relevance but queries the teacher where its target is unreliable — *"a two-sided distribution
  shift between student occupancy and teacher reliability."* Step-decaying sampling schedule emphasising
  early, lower-shift prefixes. Math-with-Python and search environments. Zero tool calls during student
  training, ≥ 4× faster per rollout.
  **Distinction for us**: the teacher prefix is a *training* device and is absent at inference. Ours
  persists at deployment. C3 is the deployment-matched analogue.

- **key**: `guided_opd_2026` — On-Policy Distillation with Curriculum Turn-level Guidance, arXiv
  2606.15912, 14 June 2026, Gengsheng Li et al. **Guided-OPD** *"mixes teacher- and student-generated
  turns within each rollout and schedules the teacher's intervention probability along a curriculum that
  decays to zero."* ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, WebShop; Qwen3-30B-A3B teacher → Qwen3 students. +21.1%
  Score, +25.5% Success Rate over vanilla OPD. Teacher is training-only: *"gradually withdrawn to
  recover the purely on-policy regime used at inference."* No AppWorld.

## D. The other direction, and fast/slow

- **key**: `swiftsage_lin_2023` — SwiftSage: A Generative Agent with Fast and Slow Thinking for Complex
  Interactive Tasks, arXiv 2305.17390, NeurIPS 2023. Swift = T5-large (770M) fine-tuned on oracle agent
  trajectories; Sage = GPT-4 for subgoal planning and grounding, invoked *on exception*. ScienceWorld,
  30 tasks; beats SayCan, ReAct, Reflexion. **Opposite direction to ours**: the small model acts by
  default and the large model is called when things break. Our action channel has the large model act
  *first*.

- **key**: `early_exit_lu_2025` — Runaway is Ashamed, But Helpful: On the Early-Exit Behavior of LLM-based
  Agents in Embodied Environments, arXiv 2505.17616, EMNLP 2025 Findings, Qingyu Lu et al. Weak agent
  exits early, **stronger** agent continues: *"a stronger agent assists after an early-exit agent,
  achieving better performance with the same total steps."* 4 LLMs × 5 embodied environments.
  Weak→strong only; exit point not swept; no fine-tuning reported.

- **key**: `isp_2024` — Interactive Speculative Planning, arXiv 2410.00079, ICLR 2025. Approximation
  agent drafts, target agent verifies, human may interrupt. Optimises **latency**, and the target always
  verifies — the small model never finishes alone.
- **key**: `dsp_guan_2025` — Dynamic Speculative Planning, arXiv 2509.01920, Sep 2025, Yilin Guan et al.
  Async online RL, joint latency+dollar objective, single tunable parameter. "Comparable efficiency to
  the fastest lossless acceleration method while reducing total cost by 30% and unnecessary cost up to
  60%." Two agent benchmarks (not named in the abstract). NOT ESTABLISHED: which benchmarks.

## E. Step / turn-level routing (the budget-allocation neighbours)

- **key**: `policy_stepwise_routing_2026` — Policy-Guided Stepwise Model Routing for Cost-Effective
  Reasoning, arXiv 2605.06116, 7 May 2026, Wenwen Si, Insup Lee, Osbert Bastani (UPenn). RL-trained
  control policy + threshold calibration decides per step to continue with the small model or escalate.
  **Math only** (GSM8K, MATH500, OmniMath) — no environments. Once escalated the trajectory continues with
  the larger model; **the small model does not resume**. GSM8K 94.5 at 2.03 FLOPs vs 94.6 for 7B-only.
  **Explicitly does NOT analyse curve shape or position effects** — results aggregate over all problems
  and steps.
- **key**: `agentic_routing_2026` — Agentic Routing: The Harness-Native Data Flywheel, arXiv 2607.11399,
  13 July 2026, TokenRhythm Technologies. *"step-level model or model-set selection during agent
  execution conditioned on the harness state."* Benchmarks DRACO, PinchBench; pools incl. Opus 4.8, GLM
  5.2, DeepSeek V4, Gemini 3 Flash. PinchBench: quality 93.14 vs 93.35 baseline at $0.0204 vs $0.2224
  (90.8% cheaper). Router is LightGBM over hand features; **no small model is fine-tuned** (named as
  future work). **Does not vary strong-model share as a single parameter** and does not compare
  decision-maker vs critic roles.
- **key**: `mtrouter_2026` — MTRouter: Cost-Aware Multi-Turn LLM Routing with History–Model Joint
  Embeddings, arXiv 2604.23530. Turn-level routing in multi-turn agent episodes from offline
  trajectories. NOT ESTABLISHED: benchmarks, numbers.
- **key**: `perf_drift_switching_2026` — Evaluating Performance Drift from Model Switching in Multi-Turn
  LLM Systems, arXiv 2603.03111 (Khraishi et al.). Single handoff induces directionally consistent drift;
  −8 to +13 pp on Multi-IF strict success, ±4 absolute F1 on CoQA. Final turn only.
- **key**: `handoff_debt_2026` — Handoff Debt: The Rediscovery Cost When Coding Agents Take Over
  Interrupted Tasks, arXiv 2606.02875 (KC & Budathoki). Interrupts at deterministic handoff points,
  freezes the repo, four handoff views (repo-only, raw trace, summary notes, structured notes); three
  successor models; context-bearing handoffs cut median agent events 20–59% and prompt tokens 42–63%
  vs repository-only takeover.
- **key**: `swe_router_2026` — SWE-Router: Routing in Multi-turn Agentic Software Engineering Tasks,
  arXiv 2607.00053 (Son et al.). Continue-or-restart decision from a partial trajectory; does not
  transfer the trajectory.

## F. Role factorisation and small-model agents

- **key**: `think_big_search_small_2026` — Think Big, Search Small: Where Capacity Matters in
  Hierarchical Search Agents? arXiv 2607.07548, 8 July 2026, Qinnan Cai, Yibo Zhao, Xiang Li.
  Factorises into delegation / execution / answer roles, **sweeps capacity along each axis** on five
  multi-hop QA benchmarks. *"capacity sensitivity is asymmetric: scaling the delegation backbone
  improves EM by ~11 points, whereas scaling the execution sub-agent moves EM by only ~2.6 points,
  identifying decomposition as the capability bottleneck."* A 1.7B executor trained by quality-filtered
  trajectory distillation matches a frontier sub-agent with **37% fewer sub-agent tokens**.
  **Closest in spirit to our "one plan is worth +19 pp" regime finding** — but static QA retrieval, not a
  stateful environment, and capacity is swept, not the *share of the episode* the strong model executes.
- **key**: `slm_agentic_belcak_2025` — Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI, arXiv
  2506.02153, Peter Belcak et al. (NVIDIA Research + Georgia Tech). Position paper: SLMs (<10B) are
  "sufficiently powerful, inherently more suitable, and necessarily more economical for many invocations
  in agentic systems"; advocates **heterogeneous** agentic systems and gives an LLM-to-SLM conversion
  algorithm. **Use as motivation, not evidence.**
- **key**: `agent_distillation_2025` — Distilling LLM Agent into Small Models with Retrieval and Code
  Tools, arXiv 2505.17612, NeurIPS 2025. "first-thought prefix" teacher-trajectory improvement +
  self-consistent action generation; 0.5B/1.5B/3B students match next-tier CoT-distilled models.
- Also for the distillation theme: AgentTuning (AgentInstruct), FireAct, Agent-FLAN, and the KD survey
  arXiv 2402.13116. NOT ESTABLISHED: exact numbers — fetch only if cited with a number.

## I. Reverse-curriculum / demonstration-prefix lineage (C3's ancestors)

- **key**: `backplay_resnick_2018` — Backplay: "Man muss immer umkehren", arXiv 1807.06919, Resnick,
  Raileanu, Kapoor, Peysakhovich, Cho, Bruna. Starts the agent near the end of a demonstration and moves
  the start backwards during training.
- **key**: `salimans_chen_2018` — Learning Montezuma's Revenge from a Single Demonstration, arXiv
  1812.03381. Curriculum starting near the end of the demonstration, moving to the beginning.
- **key**: `rfcl_2024` — Reverse Forward Curriculum Learning, ICLR 2024.
**Use**: our prefix-m sweep is the *evaluation-time* analogue of a reverse curriculum — a known idea in
RL from demonstrations, not previously applied as a deployment-time compute-allocation parameter for
LLM agents. Say exactly that; do not claim the curriculum idea itself.

## What is NOT in any of the above (the novelty perimeter, as of 2026-09-22)

1. A **quality-versus-handoff-depth curve** on a stateful tool-use suite — Handoff Tax averages over
   switch points; Reach-or-Solve fixes one point; stepwise routing aggregates.
2. A **channel contrast at matched hosted budget** — the same spend as advice versus as actions. No paper
   found prices both.
3. A **tailored open-weight receiver** for the handoff, with a zero-shot receiver control, at an 8B-vs-
   frontier capability gap.
4. **Training on handoff suffixes for a deployment where the prefix persists** (ReOPD/Guided-OPD use
   prefixes for training and drop the teacher at inference).
5. **Pre-registered non-inferiority with clustered CIs and repeated seeds** in this literature.

Write the review so a reader can check each of these five against the cited papers.
