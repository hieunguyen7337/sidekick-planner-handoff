## Use of AI tools

The author conceived the project and developed its initial research specification in a ChatGPT (OpenAI) conversation, where GPT-5.6 Thinking answered literature questions and GPT-5.6 Sol drafted a literature review and the specification; the author returned to that conversation on 17 and 20 September 2026 to test the research gap and the thesis's framing. The author directed the project's course and approved or delegated the freeze of each held-out registration and of one pre-data amendment; no held-out episode preceded those freezes. Six further agent-appended amendments, all made before the contrasts they govern were read, are disclosed with the registrations; the author approved them retrospectively on 28 September 2026, after the held-out reads; one discloses two success-rate log lines seen from a crashed planner-alone test run. AI agents did most of the remaining work. Claude Code (Anthropic; Claude Opus 5, Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1; Opus subagents) produced the detailed designs (building on that specification), held-out preregistrations, analysis code and manuscript rewrite, ran the hosted campaigns and registered reads on QUT's Aqua cluster, and reviewed and integrated the other agents' work. Google Antigravity CLI (Gemini 3.7 Flash) drafted the first preregistration, a literature review and the first preprint draft. Cursor CLI (Grok 4.6) built the harness core and trained and evaluated the executor's LoRA adapter; Cline (GLM-5.3-flash) wrote further code. OpenAI Codex CLI workers (gpt-5.6-luna, also the studied planner) did smaller tasks, including two figures and a claims-ledger audit. Every number must match a claims-ledger row citing its source; a script checked this on the development drafts, catching three numbers invented in the first draft. A bibliography audit against live records (22 September 2026) removed four fabricated references. The project history records judgement calls agents made without the author. The author takes full responsibility for this paper.
